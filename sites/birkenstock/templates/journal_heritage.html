TRADITION SINCE 1774

BIRKENSTOCK looks back on a long tradition of shoemaking: our roots go all the way to 1774, when shoemaker Johann Adam Birkenstock first registered as a "subject and shoemaker" in the church archives of Langen-Bergheim, Germany.

In 1896, master shoemaker Konrad Birkenstock developed the first contoured insole for use in shoes made by other manufacturers - the forerunner of today's footbed. In 1902 he widened the footbed to create the first flexible arch support, and by 1925 the "blue footbed" was being produced at scale and sold across Europe.

1963 marked the launch of the MADRID, the original gymnastics sandal built on the contoured footbed. The ARIZONA, with its two adjustable straps, followed in 1973 and became the defining silhouette of the brand.

The cork-latex footbed at the heart of every BIRKENSTOCK style is still made with the same core idea as in 1896: a contoured shape that supports the natural foot, letting it grip and move the way it would in sand.
